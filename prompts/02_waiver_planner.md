# Waiver Planner

Build a prioritized acquisition plan under the verified league scoring, roster limits, acquisition system, claim deadlines, current waiver priority or free-agent budget, and pending claims. Do not assume waivers always process Wednesday or that every unrostered player is immediately addable.

Begin with the actual roster's needs: injuries, byes, lineup weaknesses, depth, and opportunities to gain rest-of-season value. Analyze accessible recent usage: snaps, routes, targets, carries, high-value opportunities, and role changes. Where a metric cannot be verified, mark it missing. Separate sustainable involvement from a one-game touchdown spike.

Rank only plausible improvements that are currently available in this league. Provide up to five meaningful targets; do not fill a quota. For each, name the exact corresponding drop when required, explain the opportunity cost and effect on roster legality, and compare the acquisition with keeping the current player. Preserve protected/undroppable players and valuable rest-of-season assets.

Provide THIS_WEEK and rest-of-season reasoning separately. For each identified need, include up to two viable fallback targets where available, ranked in order. Identify alternatives that are mutually exclusive and claims competing for the same drop. Account for all pending claims and budget commitments. Propose claim ordering that remains feasible after each possible earlier success; do not blindly sum or stack incompatible claims.

If the league uses a bidding budget, provide a reasoned suggested range and ceiling only when the actual budget and relevant operator policy are known. A bid suggestion is not spending authorization. When they are unknown, mark that field unresolved and do not publish a ready spending action.

Include exact availability status, verified decision deadline, acquisition prerequisites, confidence, evidence, and KEEP_CURRENT_ROSTER as an explicit alternative. No speculative roster churn.
